    Part 2 | Section 5

    Client health beyond the dashboard

    Leadership question: What evidence tells us this relationship is healthy?

    CX360 Module 3 called it the SLA trap: SLAs tell you what you did, not what the client experienced. At senior level, the risk is bigger. A portfolio can be green on every metric while a key relationship quietly weakens.

    Lagging indicators

    Tell you what already happened: SLA, TAT, accuracy, CSAT, escalations raised. Essential, but by the time they move, the client may have made up their mind.

    Leading indicators

    Warn you early: senior client engagement, sponsor changes, tone of emails, effort to get things done (CES), new requests, competitor contact. NPS often declines 6–9 months before a client leaves.

    Section 7

    Executive relationship mapping

    Leadership question: Are we connected to the people who shape the client's strategic decisions?

    Six roles to map in every key account

    Sponsor

    Champions the partnership at the top. When they leave, the relationship is exposed.

    Economic buyer

    Signs off budgets and renewals. Often not your day-to-day contact.

    Influencers

    Shape the decision: finance, strategy, compliance, IT.

    Champions

    Speak up for you when you're not in the room.

    Detractors

    Sceptical or would prefer another vendor. Know who they are.

    Operational contacts

    Work with your teams daily. Strong here doesn't mean strong at the top.

    The single-thread risk: if your whole relationship runs through one person, you don't have a relationship with the client. You have a relationship with that person. Senior leaders build several threads at senior level.

    The value story

    Leadership question: Why should the client keep choosing us as a strategic partner?

    A vendor reports the work. A strategic partner explains what it means for the client's business and recommends what to do next. Your managers learned IMPACT in CX360 Module 3. At executive level, it becomes the backbone of every QBR and renewal conversation.

    Before and after: pick your line of business

    Which of these are value statements?

    Select every statement that tells the client what the result means for them.

    The executive QBR

    Pre-wire it

    Never surprise a senior stakeholder in the meeting. Share the headline with the sponsor and economic buyer beforehand, so the meeting confirms rather than debates.

    Lead with BLUF

    Open with what matters to them: the outcome, the risk, the decision you need. Data supports the story; it isn't the story.

    Look forward

    Spend at least a third of the time on their next 6–12 months and where you can help. That's what moves a client up the ladder.

    Renewal isn't a report

    A renewal conversation starts 9–12 months out, with the value delivered, the relationship at senior level, and a credible view of what the next contract could achieve.

    Section 10

    One accountable partner

    Leadership question: Does the client experience one accountable partner, or several disconnected teams?

    Clients don't see towers, regions or shifts. They see one company. When three teams give three different answers, the client stops trusting all of them.

    Meridian's COO asks why patient call wait times rose last week. Customer Service says AR/Collections changed a script without telling them. AR/Collections says Coding's backlog created extra calls. Coding says nobody told them about the volume spike. Each team has emailed the client separately.

    One owner

    A named senior owner for each key client, with authority across towers.

    One view of commitments

    A single, shared list of what's been promised to the client, by whom and by when.

    Clear decision rights

    Agree in advance who decides when towers' priorities conflict.

    Escalations and cross-region handoffs are covered in depth in Managing Escalations Like a Leader (STEER and the handoff checklist) and Global Cultural Alignment.

    Section 11

    Culture and governance at scale

    CX360 Module 3 gave your managers three levers: what you model, what you measure and what you recognise. At senior level, the same levers work across towers and regions, but they need structure.

    The voice-of-client loop

    Listen

    Gather feedback from every level: surveys, QBRs, informal conversations, complaints, praise.

    Decide

    Choose the few themes worth acting on across the portfolio.

    Act

    Assign owners and dates. Fix causes, not just cases.

    Close the loop

    Tell the client what changed because of what they said. Most organisations skip this step.

    Senior leadership rituals

    Monthly CX review across towers

    Not an SLA review. "How does each key client feel, and what's changed?"

    Top at-risk accounts

    A standing agenda item: the accounts where leading indicators are slipping, and who owns the recovery.

    The empty chair

    In operational reviews, ask: "If the client were sitting here, what would they say about this decision?"

    Recognise client moments

    Publicly recognise proactive updates and great recoveries, not just green metrics.

    Business measures to watch

    Renewal outlook and retention risk · relationship health trends (NPS, CES) · repeat escalations and time to recover · commitments delivered on time · corrective actions closed · client-requested improvements delivered.

    Client styles at a glance

    AnalyticalWants data, method and root cause. Send a pre-read. Show your working.
    RelationalWants to feel the partnership and know their people are looked after. Open warmly; acknowledge impact on their team.
    DriverWants the headline, the decision and the next step. Keep it short.
    VisionaryWants to know where this is going. Link results to their long-term plans.
